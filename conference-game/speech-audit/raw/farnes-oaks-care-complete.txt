I'll never forget a Saturday almost 20 years ago.
I had just been called into a stake presidency, and I hadn't yet been released as bishop.
We had five young children, a business to run and a full day of church meetings starting
at 8 a.m. and concluding with the War of Temple Night.
It was a busy time and I wanted to give my all to the Lord, but I was never sure if I
had enough to give.
At a midday leadership meeting, the presiding authority invited questions.
I gathered my courage and asked a question about an administrative matter that was troubling
me.
Instead of getting a hopeful answer, I felt like my question was dismissed.
I felt a little disheartened and unappreciated.
I questioned why I was sacrificing so much if I was going to feel this way.
I left that meeting with no desire to attend the War of Temple Night.
I called my wife, told her how discouraged I was, and said, "Let's go to dinner instead
of the temple."
Lindsay wisely responded, "No, I think you need to be in the temple we'll talk after."
So I went to the temple, but not with the best attitude.
As I entered the dressing room, rumbling to myself, I heard someone say, "How are you,
brother?"
It was the other day on eight jokes.
We had never met before, and I wasn't about to tell a member of the corner of the 12 apostles
how discouraged I felt.
So I forced a smile and said, "Everything's great."
I changed into my temple clothes, and as I walked out to my surprise, the other oaks
was waiting for me.
He said, "Brother, tell me how you are really doing.
Something about the compassion in his voice helped me open up."
The other oaks listened, expressed sincere gratitude, said some encouraging words, and
He even answered my administrative question.
Then he gave me a big hug, and in an instant I went from feeling like giving up to feeling
like I could give my whole soul in service to the Lord.
