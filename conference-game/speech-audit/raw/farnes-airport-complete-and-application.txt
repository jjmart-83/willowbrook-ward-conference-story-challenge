A few years ago, my wife and I were waiting to board a fight for a much-needed vacation.
We arrived at the airport early, and I put my earbuds in and started reading a book thankful
for a few quiet moments to myself.
Before long, I glanced up and saw a young missionary sitting across from me.
He looked frightened and anxious.
His eyes were closed, he was rocking back and forth, and tears were streaming down his
cheeks.
Clutched in his hands were his boarding pass and a digital alarm clock.
After briefly imagining the various reasons a missionary might look so anxious, I returned
to my book.
But the spirit prompted me to look again.
When I did, I saw his tear-filled eyes looking at me, pleading for help.
Were you okay? I asked. In broken, timid English, explained that he was on the way to his mission.
It was going to be his first time on an airplane alone and he was terrified. I learned he was
a long way from his home country and he was holding the alarm clock to make sure he didn't
miss the plane. Embarrassed by how slow I had been to help this elder, I explained that
my wife and I would be on the same flight and we would make sure he made it on the plane.
and I walked him to the gate and showed him the door he would go through and soon it was
our turn to board. So Lindsay and I left him at the gate and took our seats. As the plane
filled up, we anxiously watched for our missionary friend. There was no sign of him. I worried
we had made a big mistake by boarding without him. Finally, there he was, like every missionary,
last one to board. Still clutching his boarding pass an alarm clock making his way toward the
only empty seat at the back of the plane. It was the seat next to me. We smiled at each
other amazed at how God had arranged even a small detail like that one. Then the elder
finally relaxing a bit fell fast asleep. When we landed I woke him and the panic returned
to his face, as he worried about what to do next.
Where was he supposed to go?
What if there was no one waiting for him?
Lindsay and I helped him get off the plane,
walked him arm in arm not to lose him again,
all the way to the baggage claim area.
Waiting for him there were his mission leaders.
Knowing what a special moment this always is,
we were excited to see this new missionary
being greeted by his leaders for the very first time.
But to our surprise, our friend didn't run
his mission leaders. Instead, he dropped his bags and gave Lindsey and me a big hug of
luck, love and appreciation. What we did for that elder didn't require much of us, but
this ordinary encounter became a divine appointment. We will always treasure. All we had to do
was see the person God had placed in our path. Look with compassion rather than judgment and
act. Brothers and sisters, let us pray every day for eyes to see the divine
appointments God places before us. Let us put compassion into action and
minister to the person in front of us. It's what Jesus did and it's what we do.
In the sacred name we take upon ourselves by covenant, even Jesus Christ. Amen.
