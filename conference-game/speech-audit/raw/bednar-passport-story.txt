I began with a short story.
Once a man wanted to visit family members in a distant country, he planned his trip
packed his suitcase and traveled by airplane to the city where his relatives lived.
The man excitedly exited the airplane and walked to the passport control checkpoint.
He had a valid passport issued by an authorized agency of his country's government.
He presented his passport to a border official who verified the man's identity, nationality,
and reasons for visiting the country.
But the man had not taken good care of his passport.
The personal information page had been stained and damaged, and a ripped page had been repaired
in an unauthorized manner with tape and glue.
Consequently, the border official rejected his passport and refused to provide the stamp
necessary for him to enter the country.
The man was disappointed and perplexed about what he should do next.
The lesson of this story is quite simple.
The integrity of a passport must be maintained over time.
Holding a valid passport provided the opportunity for the man to enter the country.
But the passport alone did not guarantee he actually would be allowed to enter the country.
Of course, this simple analogy has limitations.
