That realization came in 1998,
when in a very weak moment, my running partner, Ted,
somehow talked me into entering a 100-mile
ultra-marathon race.
Now clearly, I was not only out of my league,
but possibly out of my mind for saying yes.
Sure, I'd run a few marathons before,
but not four of them back-to-back.
Well, everything about it seemed impossible, except for one thing.
And that was that Ted believed I could do it.
And that mattered to me because he'd completed the race himself many times, so he knew every
twist of the trail.
And he also promised that if I would put in the training that he would be there to run
with me through the second half of the race, when fatigue, blisters, and especially self-doubt
would become my worst enemies.
All race day arrived, and setting off in the pre-dawn hours, I quickly realized that this
was much bigger than me, but somehow I kept moving, even through the withering heat of
the afternoon, one slogging suffering step at a time.
And then, with more than half of the race still ahead of me, and with daylight and my
hopes fading, sure enough, Ted showed up.
Now with Ted's patience, persistence, and my unceasing silent prayers, we miraculously
ran, actually make that trudged to mile 90.
Morning had just barely dawned.
There were just ten miles remaining.
But I was completely sapped, running on empty, as it were.
Collapsing in the shade of a tree, I looked over at Ted, and quietly, but certainly said
to him, "I give up."
Even as my words and that resignation settled into the morning air, I guess I got what
you could arguably call the world's first ever TED talk.
And in a gentle reassuring tone, he reminded me of the months of preparation and those early
morning runs.
He then kneeled down, looked me in the eye, and said something I will never forget.
"I know you can do this, Mike,
"and I'm going to get you there."
Well, those simple, sincere words sparked something inside me.
And after a few more minutes, I did.
I got to my feast, and at first I walked,
and then I hobbled into, well, a pretty solid shuffle.
And somehow, step by step, together,
we conquered those last 10 miles.
The impossible now looked possible.
but about a hundred yards from the finish line.
Ted suddenly reached out and grabbed my hand.
Congrats, man, he said, you did it.
And with that, it began to peel off
even as I begged him to finish beside me.
But he simply smiled and added,
this is your race to finish, which I somehow did.
Well, a week or so later,
a photograph from the race arrived in the mail.
miraculously, a friend captured that exact moment
when Ted and I parted ways.
But it also revealed a startling secret.
You see, as I look closer,
I saw what Ted really did for me that day.
Much to my astonishment,
I saw that he wasn't only carrying his water.
He was carrying two of my water bottles and my pack,
which he dutifully toted every step of those last 10
grueling miles.
Somehow there on the side of the trail
with me in deep despair and ready to quit,
he quietly lightened my load, strapping my hydration pack
to his own waist.
And without fanfare or fuss, he simply
shouldered my heaviest burden.
