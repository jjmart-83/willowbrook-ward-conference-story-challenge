In the late 1980s, Mr. Martin developed severe heart failure.
Despite the most advanced medications available, his heart couldn't adequately support his
circulation.
He would die without a heart transplant.
He became my team's patient, and the weight for a donor heart began.
His condition worsened, and mechanical pumps were implanted to sustain his life.
A few days later, Mr. Martin's liver, kidneys, and lungs began to fail.
If a donor heart didn't become available soon, he would surely die.
One became available, and Mr. Martin received a transplant.
Surprisingly, this new heart didn't work well, and his condition was now even worse.
After a couple of days, the situation seemed hopeless, and we were preparing to withdraw
our life support. While discussing the limited options with Mrs. Martin, I was notified that
another donor heart was available. This donor heart was considered marginal and wouldn't
be used for any other recipient in the country. With Mrs. Martin's consent, we decided to
use this marginal heart for a second transplant. Fortunately, this marginal heart functioned
beautifully. Within hours, Mr. Martin's liver kidneys and lungs began to recover, and the
mechanical pumps were removed. Ten days later, I walked into Mr. Martin's hospital room knowing
that he'd be discharged from the hospital the next day. He was sitting on his bed, gripping
his breakfast tray, and was clearly irritated. I asked what was wrong. Through clenched teeth,
Mr. Martin replied, "The oatmeal isn't hot, and the milk isn't cold."
For the past three weeks, Mr. Martin had hovered near death, yet was now complaining about the
hospital food.
I think when you go through something like Mr. Martin did, you use up all your coping
coupons.
And I don't think anybody should be tortured with oatmeal, regardless of temperature.
But at that moment Mr. Martin focused on the cold oatmeal in front of him, and seemingly
dismissed the miracle he'd experienced.
He lost sight of the bigger picture, both where he had been and the promise the future
now held.
Me too, can easily fall into the same trap.
