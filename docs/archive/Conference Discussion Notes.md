## Findings from the Saturday review

- **Deacons:** Giuffra is the strongest lead for the actual assigned question about blessings/results. Include his explanation that obedience does not eliminate trials. Caussé supplies specific results of bearing witness, not just the duty to do it.
- **Builders of Faith:** retain Fale as the lead, but use his later discussion of labels and treatment of others. His opening states identity; the later passage answers the group's question. Hathaway's opening is a bridge into hardship, not evidence about divine identity.
- **Teachers:** organize the material around three separate steps: seek, recognize, act. Soares, Kearon, and the examples in Caussé/Farnes complement each other.
- **Messengers of Hope:** add Hathaway's assurance that painful questions and emotions are not sin or weakness. Runia's rock-in-a-storm image is easier to use in a youth challenge than the longer sculpting excerpt. Eyring explicitly includes resources and help from people.
- **Priests:** keep Chigbundu's sacrament teaching, then use Farnes to connect Sunday promises with Monday behavior. Gong supplies temple practice; Morgan supplies family hope while explicitly preserving agency.
- **Gatherers of Light:** Farnes supplies the clearest everyday action pattern. Use Rasband's explanation of agency and his warning that pressure can widen a rift alongside his teaching about love.
- **Broader conference material:** Christofferson's warning about gambling is a substantial youth-relevant discipleship topic, not merely a weak leftover. Sikahema supplies a useful challenge about what we do with access to prophetic teaching. They can be optional rounds.

## Group discussion notes

### Deacons — Following Jesus Christ

**Assigned question:** What blessings or results can we expect by following Him every day?

**Latest additions:** **Moisés Villanueva, 01:59–04:20**, directly examines the expectation of a particular blessing and invites noticing significant but subtle blessings instead. Keep his tithing context and distinguish his personal experiences from guarantees of health, employment, or possessions. **Paul H. Sinclair, 02:22–03:16**, connects discipleship with being quick to help and slow to judge. **Clark G. Gilbert, 11:15–11:48**, joins truth and love in discipleship. **Oaks's closing address, 00:32–00:56**, gives a concise next step: study, ponder prayerfully, and act on conference teachings.

**Sunday addition: Neil L. Andersen, 03:45–04:18.** Choosing faith in Christ brings courage to choose rightly, including in difficult situations; exercising that courage strengthens faith. This is a concrete result of daily discipleship. His **09:12–09:35** closing promise includes answers, increased spiritual power, and courage as we believe, follow, and keep commandments. It does not specify an immediate timetable for answers.

**Sunday addition: Quentin L. Cook, 02:30–03:40.** Integrity means honesty and living by our beliefs and commitments; he connects it with character, alignment with God's will, and happiness. His **05:55–07:35** discussion of online cruelty and peacemaking makes the application concrete for youth. **Alfred Kyungu, 04:07–05:13 and 05:45–06:40**, shows how following Jesus changes our language and helps us become instruments of peace. These passages invite an actual choice, not just naming a blessing.

**Saturday foundation: Patricio M. Giuffra, 03:58–04:51.** He explains that keeping commandments expresses love for God. Commandments draw us closer to the Savior, protect us, and help us remain free from sin. He then explicitly says that obedience does not mean freedom from trials: uncertainty, hardship, illness, and loss still occur, and God's promises unfold in His time and way. Keep that qualification with the blessings.

**Concrete results: Gérald Caussé, 08:35–09:10.** Bearing a sincere witness can strengthen our own testimony, draw us closer to the Lord, and make us instruments to bless others. His earlier **03:01–03:39** passage explains that disciples bear witness through words and actions. His niece's story at **06:36–07:42** helps youth see that a sincere witness can be brief and simple.

**Everyday behavior: Timothy L. Farnes, 03:04–04:09.** Taking Christ's name means reflecting His kindness, patience, hope, love, and compassion in encounters with others. This also connects directly with the Priests and Gatherers assignments.

**Supporting angle: Sean Douglas, 05:04–05:18.** A mission is one step in lifelong discipleship. Useful for connecting future plans with daily discipleship now, but less direct as an answer about present blessings.

**Ask:** Choose an ordinary decision at school or home. How could following Jesus affect both the choice and the person you become? What result might you reasonably hope for even if the situation stays hard?

**Listen for:** closeness to Christ, stronger testimony, freedom from sin, opportunities to bless others, and growing Christlike qualities. Do not turn these into a guarantee of popularity, money, or an easy life.

### Builders of Faith — Our identity and worth as children of God

**Assigned question:** How does knowing who we are change how we see ourselves and treat others?

**Latest addition: Andrea Muñoz Spannaus, 01:52–02:18; 03:35–04:09.** The Lord knows the young women's circumstances, gifts, questions, and weaknesses without loving them less. She testifies of beloved daughters of Heavenly Parents with divine nature, destiny, and meaningful individual work. Her account of Christ expressing love before inviting improvement (**02:40–03:36**) supplies a helpful application to treating others. Pair her identity passage with Chibota/Fale for both halves of the question; keep her address to young women intact when using exact wording.

**Sunday addition: Rosemary K. Chibota, 03:51–04:36; 05:05–05:52.** Worldly labels do not capture our whole identity. Being a child of God is inherited rather than achieved, and it does not disappear when circumstances change, we make mistakes, or others misunderstand us. Her school-janitor story (**02:02–02:34**) shows what this changes: a student learns his name, greets him, and helps him. Use the identity teaching and story together to answer both halves of the assignment.

**Especially direct summary: Gary E. Stevenson, 04:05–04:58.** Heavenly Father knows us personally; knowledge of divine identity makes us stronger and kinder because others are our brothers and sisters. Although much of the talk addresses Primary children, this passage answers the youth assignment plainly. **Dallin H. Oaks, 09:57–10:40**, reinforces that Christ invites all and that all are alike unto God; **Uchtdorf, 04:00–05:43**, discusses overcoming divisions and gathering God's family. These support belonging without replacing Fale's foundation.

**Foundation: Hutch U. Fale, 00:01–01:02.** He teaches that all of us are beloved children of Heavenly Parents, with divine nature and destiny. Understanding God's love gives purpose and helps protect us from deception.

**Best application passage: Fale, 05:32–06:13.** Understanding God's love changes the labels we accept for ourselves and put on other people. He describes seeing God's children as beloved and divine, becoming quick to repent and slow to judge, and making sure people are not left alone. This answers both halves of the assignment directly.

**Memorable story: Fale, 01:52–02:55.** He shows his children baby pictures and asks what they had done to earn their parents' love. The answer is nothing. He relates that love to the much greater love of Heavenly Father. His **03:26–04:06** acknowledgment that some people do not have a loving family experience is important context for a varied youth group.

**Supporting reassurance: Patrick Kearon, 03:45–04:02.** An adoring Father and a Savior who loves to lift. **Dale G. Renlund, 06:57–07:53**, tells of his sister imagining an asterisk that excluded her from God's blessings, then learning she was not an exception. This is a helpful connection about feeling excluded, rather than the primary definition of divine identity.

**Bridge only: Kevin J. Hathaway, 00:00–00:30.** Feeling left out or not enough is an application situation; the passage itself teaches Christ's deliverance. Prefer it for Messengers of Hope, or pair it explicitly with Fale.

**Ask:** Someone calls themselves a failure after one mistake; someone else labels a classmate weird. What would Fale's teaching change in the way each person is seen and treated?

**Listen for:** worth is deeper than achievement or social labels; that truth changes treatment of others and makes repentance hopeful. Fale distinguishes enduring divine love from the need to receive Christ and keep covenants (**04:06–04:58**); do not imply either that mistakes erase worth or that choices cease to matter.

### Teachers — Recognizing the Holy Ghost and receiving personal revelation

**Assigned question:** How can we seek, recognize, and act on guidance from the Spirit?

**Latest additions:** **Thabo Lebethoa, 02:04–02:45**, explains how remembering Christ's words about contention enlightens his mind and helps with a disagreement. At **05:01–06:28**, he connects sincere, consistent scripture study with enlightenment and action. **Gilbert, 06:00–06:48**, feels prompted to wait rather than interrupt, listens, and gains understanding before teaching. **Spannaus, 04:08–04:37**, invites seeking the Spirit to choose one focus and asking daily for His companionship. **Sinclair, 02:22–03:16**, describes learning through the Holy Ghost from another person's small example. These add different forms of guidance without requiring a dramatic sensation.

**Sunday addition: Rosemary K. Chibota, 06:06–06:36.** She prays about helping an isolated coworker, acts on simple promptings to include him, and sees their friendship grow. This is the clearest new everyday example of seeking and responding to guidance, and closely matches the proposed game's inclusion scenario. **Henry B. Eyring, 06:46–07:24**, similarly describes praying to know whom to help, then reaching out when a face or name comes to mind. These examples illustrate possible guidance rather than prescribing one required experience for everyone.

**Recognizing the Spirit's work: Gary E. Stevenson, 09:02–09:38.** He names warning, comfort, and testimony as roles of the Holy Ghost. This adds substance beyond a general feeling of spiritual lift. **Quentin L. Cook, 08:49–09:36**, connects integrity, repentance, and the Spirit's companionship and guidance. Cook primarily explains conditions for companionship, not an exhaustive test for whether an impression is revelation. **Oaks, 05:22–06:18**, places personal revelation alongside scripture and inspired teaching in spiritual learning.

**Seek: Ulisses Soares, 02:18–03:15.** He links fasting with intentional prayer, humility, seeking God's will, softened hearts, and greater receptiveness to the Holy Ghost and personal revelation. At **04:27–05:24**, he explains alignment with God's purposes and accepting His wisdom when answers differ from expectations. Introduce the subject of fasting if using only the short passage about revelation.

**Recognize and act: Patrick Kearon, 04:54–05:20; 09:21–10:12.** His gliding analogy describes unseen lift that can be felt. He connects it with discerning and acting on impressions of the Holy Spirit. His closing suggestions include learning from a faithful guide, attending to the Spirit, joining those who are rising, and helping someone else rise. The analogy illustrates guidance; it is not a complete rule that every pleasant feeling must be revelation.

**Personal example: Gérald Caussé, 00:18–01:01.** He prays for guidance, feels prompted to open a magazine, reads a reassuring scripture, and receives peace. The caption misspells the magazine's name; use a paraphrase unless audio confirms it.

**Action example: Timothy L. Farnes, 05:25–06:42; 08:21–08:48.** He initially returns to his book after noticing a distressed missionary, then feels prompted to look again and offers practical help. This makes acting on a prompting understandable without requiring a dramatic event.

**Related but distinct: Matthew J. Eyring, 07:08–07:33.** The Holy Ghost comforts us in hardships. It belongs in this conversation about the Spirit, but the excerpt primarily answers the Hope topic rather than how to recognize guidance.

**Ask:** For a decision or a kind action this week, what could seeking, noticing, and responding look like? What could you do if you are uncertain?

**Listen for:** making room for guidance, responding thoughtfully, and taking a constructive next step. Let youth discuss hypothetical situations; no one needs to prove that they have had a particular spiritual sensation.

### Messengers of Hope — Finding strength in Christ through challenges

**Assigned question:** What can we do when life is hard or our faith is strained?

**Latest additions:** **Spannaus, 05:53–07:45**, describes feeling inadequate while teaching in a new language and receiving reassurance of divine help. **Gabriel W. Reid, 07:15–08:20**, teaches that complete knowledge, skill, and confidence are not prerequisites for beginning to serve; the Savior helps us grow. **Villanueva, 01:59–04:20**, addresses weakened confidence when an expected outcome has not come. **Lebethoa, 02:44–03:37 and 06:25–07:58**, offers light, hope, repentance, and Christ's help amid daily struggles. Use these alongside Oaks/Hathaway's patience and grace; do not turn hope into a guarantee of immediate changes in external circumstances.

**Sunday addition for strained faith: Dallin H. Oaks, 02:30–04:29.** He explicitly addresses concerns, questions, and doubts. He recommends coming closer to Christ through daily prayer and scriptural learning, being patient with ourselves and others, building humility, and trusting God's timing. This strengthens coverage of the faith-strained half of the assignment, beyond coping with loss or illness. His description of resolving uncertainty as a potentially lengthy process is important to keep.

**Sunday addition for challenges: Henry B. Eyring, 01:25–02:08; 05:09–05:49.** The promises of help and strength do not promise removal of challenges. Help can come through faithful people. **Edward Dube, 01:43–02:51 and 05:49–06:15**, teaches that we come to Christ to be perfected rather than perfect ourselves before coming. That is a hopeful response to shame or feeling not good enough; it is not a claim that effort, repentance, or ordinance requirements cease to matter. **David A. Bednar, 02:36–02:58**, explicitly says adversity through no fault of our own does not diminish God's promises or invalidate covenants.

**Lead teaching: Kevin J. Hathaway, 01:46–02:43.** His pattern moves from opposition and difficult questions to sincere seeking, enabling grace, and deliverance in the Savior's time and way. He explicitly states that heart-rending questions and deep emotions are not sin or weakness. At **02:40–03:16**, seeking includes prayer, fasting, scripture study, and keeping covenants.

**Strength while waiting: Hathaway, 04:49–05:25.** Christ's grace gives strength beyond our own without necessarily removing pain. The story behind this passage concerns a missionary's father's death; introduce that context briefly rather than turning grief into a guessing exercise. At **07:03–07:34**, he distinguishes immediate miracles, long waiting, and deliverance that awaits resurrection.

**Best short visual: Tamara W. Runia, 02:00–02:36.** The gospel gives a solid foundation during storms rather than preventing every storm. At **03:36–04:01**, she distinguishes not understanding from not believing. Her Lego illustration (**04:24–05:12**) and sculpting story (**06:23–07:29**) are additional options, but need enough setup to make sense.

**Help can come through people: Michael A. Dunn, 03:50–05:32; 05:49–06:35.** His running companion quietly carries some of his load. The Savior can strengthen us and enlist others to help us keep going. **Matthew J. Eyring, 02:30–03:17**, specifically includes tools, resources, and help from other people. His **07:08–07:33** passage addresses comfort from the Holy Ghost.

**Trust and perspective: Dale G. Renlund, 04:30–05:41; 06:25–06:57.** Remember God's constancy, past mercies, and promises. His cold-oatmeal story is memorable, but do not use it to dismiss someone's real pain or accuse them of ingratitude.

**Ask:** A friend says, “I've been trying to do the right things, but this is still hard. Maybe God has forgotten me.” What could you say, and what practical support could you offer this week?

**Listen for:** acknowledge pain, allow questions, turn toward Christ, seek help, and avoid promising a quick resolution. Using a fictional example lets youth participate without disclosing grief or family troubles.

### Priests — Covenants, the sacrament, and temple preparation

**Assigned question:** How do our covenants connect us to Jesus Christ and help us become more like Him?

**Latest additions:** **Spannaus, 04:18–04:37 and 07:20–07:36**, connects sacrament humility and baptismal covenant faithfulness with the Spirit and the Lord's guidance. **Oaks's closing address, 02:30–03:05**, testifies of the covenant path toward the destiny Heavenly Father desires. The temple celebration information in its middle section offers a way to learn about the temple but little new instruction about keeping ordinances or covenants. Schmeil, Farnes, Chigbundu, and Gong remain stronger leads for the complete assignment.

**Strongest new overview: Ciro Schmeil, 02:27–04:14.** Covenants bind us to Christ and help us become yoked with Him. He connects baptismal promises with the sacrament, willingness to take Christ's name, remember Him and keep commandments, and the promised Spirit and strength to continue. At **05:05–05:17**, he directly connects the sacrament with preparation to make and keep temple covenants. This makes him an especially complete Sunday match for the assignment.

**Becoming like Christ: James G. O. Fantone, 02:03–03:30; 06:29–07:37.** Taking Christ's name involves covenant keeping and lifelong service rather than merely wearing an affiliation. He links serving others with a change of heart. **Dieter F. Uchtdorf, 02:05–03:10; 09:38–11:20**, adds that covenants bring us into a people who bear burdens and that the sacrament renews our relationship with God alongside one another.

**Deeper covenant reassurance: David A. Bednar, 02:36–04:30 and 10:36–11:18.** He explains ordinance/covenant commitments and ongoing faithfulness. His passport analogy includes an explicit limit: adversity through no fault of our own does not invalidate covenants. He also teaches that another person's wrongful choices cannot ultimately deprive a faithful disciple of promised eternal blessings, while saying he cannot explain every detail of how and when. Keep those qualifications; the later marriage discussion is a deeper pastoral topic, not the first game puzzle for youth.

**Sacrament foundation: Christian C. Chigbundu, 05:58–06:42.** The sacrament strengthens our relationship with the Savior. He names taking Christ's name, remembering Him, and keeping His commandments, then asks what Christ would have us do. Remembering Him during the week can refine and reshape our lives.

**Best bridge to ordinary life: Timothy L. Farnes, 03:04–04:09.** Taking Christ's name means reflecting His qualities; the promised Spirit helps us minister. We make the covenant in sacred time on Sunday and keep it by ministering every day. Pair this with Chigbundu so the discussion moves from naming promises to living them.

**Temple preparation and practice: Chigbundu, 06:41–07:39; Gerrit W. Gong, 06:13–07:22.** Chigbundu links covenant bonds, temple worship, and direction from the Lord. Gong discusses attending the temple and bringing family names, then emphasizes that everything in the temple testifies of Christ. Choose age-appropriate ways to prepare, learn family history, and participate in temple service available to youth. Gong's **03:58–04:43** explanation of the Holy Spirit of Promise is a useful deeper discussion, but more technical than the best opening game clip.

**Family hope: Jeremiah J. Morgan, 04:13–04:59; 06:00–07:29.** Covenants can have an impact across generations. He explicitly says faithful parents' covenants do not alter descendants' agency. Keep that qualification when discussing the reach of covenant love; do not present sealing as forcing a person's decisions or guaranteeing an outcome on our timetable.

**Supporting connections:** Hathaway's account at **04:09–04:35** connects holding to covenants with grief. Giuffra at **05:51–06:18** connects sacrifice and temple covenants, although the captions here are especially garbled. Renlund at **04:30–04:46** emphasizes God's constancy.

**Ask:** If you remember Jesus on Sunday, what might change in the way you treat someone on Monday? What is one practical way to prepare now for temple worship?

**Listen for:** a continuing relationship with Christ, help from the Spirit, and becoming more like Him through everyday choices. Covenant promises are more than a checklist or a transaction for immediate rewards.

### Gatherers of Light — Loving, serving, and inviting others to Christ

**Assigned question:** How can we help others feel the Savior's love?

**Latest additions:** **Sinclair, 01:48–03:16**, contrasts irritation at a child's mistake with another child's practical help, then explains being quick to help and slow to judge. **Reid, 04:06–04:44**, recalls teachers knowing children personally and by name; their love helped them feel the Savior's love. **Gilbert, 04:50–05:26 and 06:00–06:48**, explains the cost of an angry retort and the value of listening before responding. His wider teaching holds love and conviction together. These deepen Chibota/Farnes's pattern: notice the person, resist judgment, listen, and offer useful help. A sincere invitation should grow from care rather than winning an argument.

**Strongest new school example: Rosemary K. Chibota, 02:02–02:34; 06:35–07:16.** Her daughter greets a school janitor, knows his name, and helps clean up after lunch; he feels seen. She invites listeners to learn the name of someone who may feel unseen and pray to see others as Heavenly Father does. This gives youth an immediate, feasible action. Her opening contrasts labels with seeing beloved children of God (**00:20–01:09**).

**Belonging and invitation: Dieter F. Uchtdorf, 02:05–03:10; 11:00–11:42.** Baptism joins us to a people who bear burdens, mourn, and comfort; his invitation to come, help, and belong is grounded in a community that would be incomplete without the person. **Gary E. Stevenson, 09:49–10:49**, connects loving, sharing, and inviting through words and actions with joy for ourselves and others. **Edward Dube, 07:39–08:29**, gives a direct invitation to help one person come to Christ.

**Peaceful language and practical care: Alfred Kyungu, 04:07–05:13**, offers words that preserve relationships and communicate care. **Henry B. Eyring, 05:09–07:24**, explains how people can help fulfill the Lord's promise of comfort. **Fantone, 03:24–05:12**, illustrates service through his parents making room for others. The youth application can be an ordinary act of help; it need not copy an adult family's housing decision.

**Lead pattern: Timothy L. Farnes, 04:26–04:50; 08:21–08:48.** Notice a person, respond with compassion, then act. An apparent interruption can be an opportunity to minister. The airport story (**05:25–08:22**) illustrates concrete help for an anxious person, but summarize most of it rather than using a three-minute game clip.

**Love and agency: Ronald A. Rasband, 00:58–01:43; 04:45–05:44.** He acknowledges that lectures, arguments, and other pressure can deepen a rift; people must choose for themselves. His later passage describes interest in people's lives, open arms, patience, forgiveness, and kindness. At **09:25–09:57**, he urges us not to let a problem become more important than a person to love. These are useful ways to keep invitations caring.

**Witness and love: Gérald Caussé, 05:16–06:35; 09:33–10:35.** Sharing testimony flows from love, and expressing Christlike love bears witness of Him. Brief, sincere expressions of belief can occur naturally in daily interactions. His niece's simple testimony (**06:36–07:42**) makes this approachable.

**Additional connections:** Fale's **05:32–06:13** passage directly connects divine identity with leaving no one alone. Soares at **05:23–07:41** connects worship and generosity with care for people in need. Douglas at **00:52–01:18** emphasizes living examples as well as messages. Kearon at **08:46–09:10** discusses helping others find spiritual lift.

**Ask:** A friend declines an invitation to church or an activity. What could you do next that keeps the relationship warm and helps them feel loved?

**Listen for:** continue friendship, listen, offer meaningful help, make invitations natural, and allow a real choice. Invitations can be an expression of love; acceptance is not a condition for receiving it.
