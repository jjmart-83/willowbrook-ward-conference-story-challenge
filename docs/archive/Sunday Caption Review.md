# Sunday captions reviewed against the fireside assignments

**Reviewed:** October 4, 2026. **Scope:** the first batch of 12 Sunday talks, read in full from the supplied English SRT captions. Together with the Saturday review, this batch brought the reviewed total to 30. Seven subsequently added Sunday addresses are documented in [Additional Talk Review.md](Additional%20Talk%20Review.md), bringing the current total to **37 addresses by 36 speakers**. The user confirmed that 12 was the correct count for this first Sunday batch.

The source assignment is [Fireside topics.docx.md](Fireside%20topics.docx.md). Updated group notes and the complete 37-address map are in [Conference Discussion Notes.md](Conference%20Discussion%20Notes.md).

These notes paraphrase the captions. Windows refer to original-video locations, not prepared clips or final cuts. Both caption copies for each new talk are identical, and cues overlap. This establishes useful content connections but does not verify exact wording against audio. No new Sunday excerpt cards or clips were created during this review.

## What Sunday adds

Sunday particularly strengthens **identity expressed through treatment of others**, **covenants as a source of strength and belonging**, and **patience when faith is uncertain**. There are also concrete youth applications: Chibota's school-janitor story, Kyungu's replacement phrases, Cook's discussion of online bullying, and Andersen's connection between faithful choices and courage.

The Teachers assignment benefits from using several passages together: Soares on preparing to receive guidance (Saturday), Stevenson on the Spirit's work, and Chibota or Henry B. Eyring on seeking and acting. A single passage need not provide a complete method for all three steps.

## All 12 talks: fit and useful passages

### Alfred Kyungu

**Direct:** Deacons and Gatherers. **Supporting:** Builders, through the harm done by humiliating labels.

- **00:33–01:25:** choosing peaceful language is part of following Christ and acting on prophetic teaching.
- **03:10–04:10:** examples of damaging expressions and their effect on love, respect, and relationships.
- **04:07–05:13:** replacement language that expresses respect, care, patience, apology, and forgiveness.
- **05:45–06:40:** Christ's teaching changes our hearts and language; disciples can become instruments of peace.

**Use:** ask teams to rewrite a fictional harsh message while preserving the concern it needs to address. This is a stronger ordinary-life task than simply naming the speaker. Make clear that respectful speech can still express disagreement or a boundary; the task is not to pretend a problem does not exist.

### Ciro Schmeil

**Direct:** Priests, Hope, and Deacons. **Supporting:** Teachers, through the promised Spirit and seeking God's will.

- **00:22–01:50:** Christ and covenants help with weakness; humility and faith include trust when answers are incomplete.
- **02:27–03:36:** covenants bind us to Christ; the yoke image connects that relationship with carrying burdens.
- **03:32–04:14:** remembering Christ, willingness to keep sacrament promises, the promised Spirit, and strength along the covenant path.
- **05:05–05:17:** the sacrament prepares us to make and keep temple covenants.
- **06:22–07:24:** consider our ways, strengthen our relationship with God through covenants, and remember our need for the Savior.

**Use:** the strongest new overview for Priests. Pair the short sacrament passage with the temple-preparation sentence or a host paraphrase. His teaching about willingness rather than perfection should remain connected with sincere covenant keeping; do not extrapolate it into a complete account of participation standards.

### Dallin H. Oaks

**Direct passages:** Hope, Deacons, Teachers, Priests, Builders, and Gatherers. The most useful new focus is questions and doubts.

- **02:30–04:29:** daily prayer and scriptural learning, patience with ourselves and others, humility, and trusting God's timing when uncertainty takes time to resolve.
- **05:22–06:18:** spiritual learning includes faith, scripture, inspired teaching, and personal revelation; learning by spiritual and scientific methods need not ultimately conflict.
- **07:04–07:34:** sacrament renewal and living covenants as protective practices; temple attendance is addressed to those endowed.
- **09:57–10:40:** Christ invites all; all are alike unto God.
- **12:22–13:10:** act on and share these truths to draw closer to Christ.

**Use:** especially important for the Hope group's original wording, “our faith is strained.” Do not reduce the passage to a promise that a question will disappear immediately or treat uncertainty as evidence of a person's failure.

### David A. Bednar

**Direct:** Priests and Hope. **Supporting:** Teachers through the Holy Spirit of Promise, a role distinct from the personal-revelation question.

- **00:00–02:38:** ordinances and covenants as spiritual credentials, illustrated by a passport.
- **02:36–02:58:** explicit limit of the analogy: adversity through no fault of our own does not diminish God's promises or invalidate covenants.
- **02:54–04:30:** covenant commitments, priesthood ordinances, divine direction and strength, and ongoing faithfulness.
- **04:30–06:35:** the Holy Spirit of Promise and covenant ratification over time.
- **08:25–11:18:** another person's wrongful choices do not ultimately deprive a faithful disciple of promised eternal blessings; he acknowledges that not every detail of how and when is revealed.

**Use:** an optional deeper Priests discussion. If using the passport image, include its limit so damaged documents are not equated with being harmed or experiencing hardship. The later marital questions deserve thoughtful context and should not be a rapid youth scoring puzzle.

### Dieter F. Uchtdorf

**Direct:** Priests, Gatherers, Deacons, and Builders through God's family and belonging. **Supporting:** Hope through community support.

- **00:53–01:59:** spiritual growth and deepening discipleship over time.
- **02:05–03:10:** baptism joins individuals to a covenant people who bear burdens, mourn, and comfort.
- **04:00–05:43:** Christ crosses divisions to gather God's family; isolation works against that gathering.
- **07:28–08:48:** coming closer to Christ brings us closer to one another; patience and kindness reduce contention.
- **09:38–11:20:** the sacrament renews our relationship with God in a community of disciples; we have Christ and one another.
- **11:00–11:42:** invitation to come, help, and belong; the community would be incomplete without the person.

**Use:** connect the Priests and Gatherers assignments rather than treating covenants as only private promises. Captions repeatedly render “sacrament” as “segment”; paraphrase those passages until audio confirms display text.

### Edward Dube

**Direct:** Deacons, Hope, and Gatherers. **Supporting:** Priests through the temple story and Teachers through prayer.

- **00:00–00:45:** a gate sign is removed to avoid an impression of exclusion; the invitation is to come to Christ.
- **01:43–02:51:** come, surrender what holds us back, and be perfected in Christ; we do not first perfect ourselves before coming.
- **03:00–06:15:** prayer and support for a man trying to change, including his wife's reassurance and the Savior's grace.
- **06:15–07:39:** challenges, shame, and uncertain faith need not become a barrier to turning toward Christ.
- **07:39–08:29:** invite one person toward Christ through faithful action or meeting missionaries.

**Use:** a hopeful response to “I'm not good enough to turn to Him.” Keep repentance, willing effort, and grace together. The brief perfection statement does not establish that every ordinance has identical requirements.

### Gary E. Stevenson

**Direct:** Builders, Teachers, Gatherers, Hope, and Deacons. Much of the talk addresses Primary children, but the teachings are relevant to youth.

- **01:04–01:55:** young people are known, loved, gifted, and able to bless others.
- **04:05–04:58:** divine identity makes us stronger and kinder because others are our brothers and sisters.
- **07:34–08:26:** the plan of happiness, Christ's Atonement, repentance, forgiveness, and continued progress when discouraged.
- **09:02–09:38:** the Holy Ghost warns, comforts, and testifies.
- **09:49–10:49:** love, share, and invite through words and actions; helping others partake brings joy.

**Use:** a particularly concise answer to both halves of Builders' question. The fruit image can support a simple teaching-to-action task. Do not make the youth activity dependent on a Primary song knowledge quiz.

### Henry B. Eyring

**Direct:** Hope, Gatherers, and Teachers. **Supporting:** Priests through bearing burdens and the baptismal passage.

- **01:25–02:08:** promises of aid and strength do not promise removal of challenges.
- **03:29–04:09:** Christ understands and carries grief; individuals' challenges differ.
- **05:09–05:49:** faithful people help fulfill the Lord's promises; God often meets needs through another person.
- **06:46–07:24:** prayer about whom to help, a face or name coming to mind, reaching out, and simple attention offering comfort.
- **09:13–10:35:** seek the Spirit's help to comfort and bear burdens.

**Use:** connect receiving help with becoming someone who helps. His hymn-based opening includes transcription errors; use a labeled paraphrase or check the audio before reproducing lyrics.

### James G. O. Fantone

**Direct:** Priests, Deacons, and Gatherers. **Supporting:** Hope through the missionaries' discouragement and growth.

- **00:00–02:32:** a form-signing mistake introduces the contrast between adding a name and taking Christ's name through covenant keeping.
- **02:37–03:30:** lifelong determination to serve Christ is expressed in serving people.
- **03:24–05:12:** his parents' hospitality makes room for people in need.
- **05:20–07:37:** serving missionaries change, and taking Christ's name through service can produce a change of heart.

**Use:** match a covenant promise with a feasible act of service. Keep the meaning of taking Christ's name tied to a changed life, rather than reducing it to a name-tag prop. Youth can apply the family example through ordinary help without imitating an adult housing decision.

### Neil L. Andersen

**Direct:** Deacons, Hope, Priests, and Teachers through stillness, prayer, and quiet impressions. **Supporting:** Gatherers through ministering and speaking about Christ.

- **01:27–02:05:** discipleship requires courage amid obstacles, doubt, and fear.
- **03:45–04:18:** stillness nourishes faith; faith brings courage to choose rightly, and courageous right choices strengthen faith.
- **04:18–05:31:** quiet personal prayer, sacrament promises, courageous covenant keeping, and increased faith.
- **06:25–07:24:** trusting during illness, financial difficulty, or disaster; a family facing loss of their home.
- **09:12–09:35:** believing, following, and keeping commandments bring answers, spiritual power, and courage.

**Use:** help Deacons name results that can exist during difficulty, not only after it ends. Courage is compatible with needing support; the talk is not a requirement to hide fear.

### Quentin L. Cook

**Direct:** Deacons, Teachers, and Gatherers. **Supporting:** Priests through integrity in covenant commitments.

- **02:30–03:40:** integrity means honesty and living beliefs and commitments; character, alignment with God's will, and happiness.
- **05:55–07:35:** online bullying, damaging communication, and choosing Christlike peacemaking.
- **08:49–09:36:** integrity, caring service, repentance, and the companionship and guidance of the Holy Ghost.
- **09:38–10:53:** a former sailor's joy at learning about repentance and forgiveness.

**Use:** a group-chat situation, followed by how a person could repair harm. Distinguish his teaching about companionship from a complete checklist for recognizing a particular prompting.

### Rosemary K. Chibota

**Direct:** Builders, Gatherers, Teachers, and Deacons. **Supporting:** Hope through grace and inadequacy.

- **00:20–01:09:** Christ sees beloved children rather than labels, including a person who walks away.
- **02:02–02:34:** her daughter greets a school janitor, knows his name, and helps after lunch; he feels seen.
- **03:51–04:36:** worldly labels cannot capture us fully; divine identity is inherited rather than achieved.
- **05:05–05:52:** Christ can lift us above labels; identity survives changed circumstances, mistakes, and misunderstanding.
- **06:06–06:36:** pray about an isolated coworker, act on simple promptings to include him, and develop friendship.
- **06:35–07:16:** learn someone's name, pray to see people as God does, and replace worldly labels with eternal truths.

**Use:** especially strong for the activity's intended ages because her examples are small and concrete. Builders and Gatherers can interpret the same story from different angles. The opening greeting is garbled in the captions; avoid printing its spelling as verified.

## Implications for the game concept

Keep the six-team, six-mission structure. Sunday improves the available evidence without requiring all 30 talks to appear in the game. Chibota offers a real example for the inclusion mission; Schmeil gives a strong sacrament passage; Kyungu gives language-repair challenges; Oaks gives a better anchor for strained faith. The proposed source options and the evidence questions that would change are recorded in [Conference Game Proposal.md](Conference%20Game%20Proposal.md).

The browser and original 20-card deck still use the prototype content. Final clip selection, audio checks, and any runtime redesign are separate implementation steps.
