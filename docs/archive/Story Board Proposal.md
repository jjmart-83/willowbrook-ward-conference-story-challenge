## Why the stories fit this format

A picture, a short retelling, or a clip can bring the story back to mind. The question then moves from remembering an object or action to understanding a teaching and applying it. Names and speaker portraits serve as attribution. The six fireside assignments give the board its structure, while the story tags let the host find a mix of personal experiences, family stories, accounts of others, history, and scripture.

I recommend **topic columns with story-type tags**. Using only “Family” or “History” as columns would be possible, but those headings would not ensure balanced coverage of the assignments. Keep the five tags available as filters and small badges on clues.

## Categories and difficulty

| Column | Assigned group | What the harder clues test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Follow Christ | Deacons | Daily choices, consequences, and realistic expectations of blessings |
| Know Your Worth | Builders of Faith | Divine identity and how it changes treatment of self and others |
| Find Guidance | Teachers | Seeking, recognizing, and acting on guidance |
| Find Strength | Messengers of Hope | Receiving Christ's help while a challenge remains |
| Keep Promises | Priests | How covenants connect us with Christ and shape actions |
| Help Others | Gatherers of Light | Seeing, listening, loving, serving, and inviting |

| Value | Question pattern | Suggested answer time |
| ---: | --- | ---: |
| 100 | Recognize a clear object, action, or simple fact | 20 seconds |
| 200 | Recall two details or explain a direct meaning | 25 seconds |
| 300 | Explain a connection or make a simple application | 35 seconds |
| 400 | Distinguish an overclaim, compare ideas, or handle a limit | 45 seconds |
| 500 | Apply the teaching to a situation with two or three required parts | 60 seconds |

Difficulty should come from the reasoning required, rather than obscure dates, names, or how sad a story is. The values below are provisional: play a few clues with a leader or small youth group and adjust if a clue is noticeably easier or harder than its row.

## Keep all six teams involved

With 30 participants, teams average five; with 50, they average eight or nine. Use the assigned groups when reasonably balanced, or balance team sizes while retaining their assigned expertise. Start with pairs or trios for discussion, then agree on a written team answer. Rotate the writer, evidence finder, and spokesperson.

The selecting team chooses a category and value. **Every team answers the same clue** on paper or a small whiteboard and reveals together. The choosing team receives the same scoring treatment as the others. Selection rotates through the teams regardless of whether the last answer was correct. This keeps everyone participating without relying on fast buzzers.

For the first six picks, each team opens its assigned category. During the next six, choose the remaining categories so each column has been played twice. Then open selection across the board. A 50-minute version can stop after 18 clues, with at least two from each topic; it does not require clearing all 30 squares.

An image-only recall clue is appropriate when the image is intentionally helpful. For other clues, read a 15–25-second paraphrase or play a short, audio-checked excerpt before the question. This gives youth who listened for another assignment enough evidence to participate. Many full stories are several minutes long: use a short retelling plus an excerpt, rather than playing every whole story.

## Scoring and the host key

- **Full value:** correct meaning; all required parts for a multi-part question.
- **Half value:** a substantially correct but incomplete answer on a multi-part clue. All values divide evenly by two.
- **Zero:** incorrect, blank, or unsupported. No point deductions.
- Accept equivalent words. Exact quotations and speaker names are not required.
- For an open application, award the points for the listed criteria, not for matching one sample sentence. Two responses can both be sound.
- When asking “which fireside topic?”, require a topic, a specific story detail, and an explanation. Several topics may fit. The selected column supplies one useful connection; it does not make other supported connections wrong.

Teams self-check against the revealed key while two circulating adults help with unclear answers. The host settles disputed responses consistently and can correct the score. There are no steals, speed bonuses, elimination, or wagers. A tie can remain shared.

## Timing and materials

**50-minute default:** 5 minutes for teams/rules and a practice clue; 36 minutes for 18 selections at roughly two minutes each; 5 minutes for a cooperative final connection; 4 minutes for scores and a private next step. Show the remaining time and stop at the planned break. If a longer discussion is useful, play fewer clues while preserving the topic coverage.

A **60–70-minute version** can attempt the full 30-clue board; its pace depends on media length and how much discussion the host allows. A **40-minute version** can use 12 selections, two per category, followed by the cooperative finish.

Use one laptop/TV with sound, pencils and response sheets or whiteboards, six topic cards, and a visible selection tracker. No participant devices or accounts are needed. The caption-based story summary is the backup if a video is unavailable.

For the final connection, show a familiar story image and ask every team to support **two different fireside connections** with evidence. Share a few examples without adding points. Close with Oaks's **00:32–00:56 study → ponder prayerfully → act in faith** invitation from `dallin-h-oaks-temples`. Youth can choose a private next step.

## Questions and host answers

The catalog summary supplies the source context for each clue. These keys are paraphrases based on captions. Verify the selected media before final use, particularly source names and exact boundaries. This is a selection bank, not a requirement to show all 30 stories in one evening.

### Follow Christ

**100 — Peaches at the farm stand** · [Story and prompt](../../Conference%20Story%20Catalog.md#stevenson-fruit)

What fruit was the family's favorite farm stand especially known for?

- Peaches.

**200 — A plan that helped him stay** · [Story and prompt](../../Conference%20Story%20Catalog.md#chigbundu-missionary)

What plan did the new missionary make with his trainer, and what changed later?

- A scripture-study plan, especially the Book of Mormon.
- He understood his purpose, felt happier, and wanted to stay.

**300 — Act well thy part** · [Story and prompt](../../Conference%20Story%20Catalog.md#cook-mckay)

A student has a small role in a school project and thinks it does not matter. Apply McKay's lesson with one specific action.

- Do the assigned part as well as possible.
- Name a concrete action: prepare, finish honestly, help the group, or a comparable example.

**400 — The missing promotion** · [Story and prompt](../../Conference%20Story%20Catalog.md#villanueva-promotion)

A friend says obedience must bring the outcome they want. Identify the mistaken expectation and suggest a better response using this story.

- Obedience does not guarantee a particular promotion or our preferred timetable.
- Notice other help or blessings while honestly acknowledging disappointment.

**500 — A name that changes our actions** · [Story and prompt](../../Conference%20Story%20Catalog.md#fantone-name)

Explain the difference between adding Christ's name as a label and taking His name through covenant. Give a realistic action that shows the second.

- A label alone does not mean becoming like Christ.
- Covenant discipleship involves an ongoing determination to serve Him.
- Give a specific act of faithful or loving service linked to the teaching.

### Know Your Worth

**100 — The baby pictures** · [Story and prompt](../../Conference%20Story%20Catalog.md#fale-photos)

What had the children done as babies to earn their parents' love?

- Nothing; their parents already loved them.

**200 — More than hello** · [Story and prompt](../../Conference%20Story%20Catalog.md#chibota-greeting)

What does I see you communicate beyond a greeting? Name two meanings.

- Any two: recognition, respect, human worth, concern, belonging, or you matter.

**300 — The right location** · [Story and prompt](../../Conference%20Story%20Catalog.md#spannaus-gps)

How does the GPS experience illustrate the Lord's knowledge of us? What does she say about Him knowing our weaknesses?

- The Lord knows where we actually are and our circumstances, unlike the confused GPS.
- Knowing our weaknesses and questions does not make Him love us less.

**400 — Everyone except me?** · [Story and prompt](../../Conference%20Story%20Catalog.md#renlund-asterisk)

Someone thinks scriptural promises exclude them personally. What does Linda's lesson challenge? Encourage them without promising an instant result.

- Challenge the imagined personal exclusion: God's promises apply to them too.
- Offer support, trust, or a faithful step while respecting the promises' conditions and God's timing.

**500 — Love before improvement** · [Story and prompt](../../Conference%20Story%20Catalog.md#spannaus-young-man)

Your friend made a mistake and calls themself worthless. Use Christ's response to write two sentences supporting both worth and growth.

- Affirm worth and being known and loved without requiring perfection.
- Encourage a constructive next step or repair without shame.
- Connect to Christ's love preceding His invitation to improve.

## Story selection and image preparation

The sample board emphasizes concrete actions and connections. The larger catalog also includes difficult grief and loss accounts; choose those for a thoughtful discussion where they serve the group, rather than making the tragedy's details into trivia. Preserve the context of tithing accounts and Bednar's explicit limit to the passport analogy. Likewise, a gliding analogy illustrates spiritual guidance but does not establish that every pleasant feeling is revelation.

Each catalog prompt describes an illustrative scene. Keep one visual style across the board. If the prompt depicts the answer itself—for example, Ted carrying the pack—use it on the reveal or ask a meaning/application question instead of asking what the picture plainly shows. For recognition clues, that visual help can be intentional.

Repeated scripture stories have recurring-story keys in the data. Avoid selecting two accounts of the same incident unless the question deliberately compares what the speakers emphasize. Images of scriptural parables, literary fiction, and imagined scenes should retain that attribution in the host notes.

## What the browser build would need

Keep the source inventory, captions, speaker cards, and prep workflow. Replace the current two-team speaker quiz with:

1. A six-column board, values 100–500, and cleared-square indicators.
2. Up to six editable team names and scores; rotating selection and coverage tracking.
3. A clue screen with optional image, source attribution, short host context, question, replay, and timer.
4. A separate host/reveal view for answer criteria, accepted topic connections, and manual full/half/zero awards.
5. A return-to-board action, score correction, save/resume, and a closing screen.
